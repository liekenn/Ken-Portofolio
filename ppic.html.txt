<section class="chapter" id="context"><div class="sectitle"><span>01</span><h2>From demand to capacity</h2></div><p>The lecturer-provided manufacturing case required a coordinated plan from forecasting to material requirements and work-center loading. The planning horizon for the master production schedule was January–March 2026.</p><div class="steps"><div><b>01</b>Forecasting</div><div><b>02</b>Aggregate planning</div><div><b>03</b>MPS & RCCP</div><div><b>04</b>MRP & lot-sizing</div><div><b>05</b>CRP</div></div><div class="tags"><span>Microsoft Excel</span><span>Solver</span><span>Forecast error metrics</span><span>BOM</span><span>Capacity analysis</span></div><aside class="note">All figures are academic model outputs, not operational results or realized company savings.</aside>

<section class="chapter" id="forecast"><div class="sectitle"><span>02</span><h2>Demand forecasting</h2></div><p>Compared three methods on 30 monthly observations (July 2023–December 2025). Cyclical regression was selected for the lowest mean absolute deviation (MAD) and mean squared error (MSE), despite Holt-Winters having a marginally lower mean absolute percentage error (MAPE).</p><div class="tablewrap"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Method</th><th scope="col">MAD</th><th scope="col">MSE</th><th scope="col">MAPE</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Holt-Winters seasonal</td><td>551.07</td><td>604,707.16</td><td>5.7223%</td></tr><tr><td>Exponential smoothing (Pegels)</td><td>691.99</td><td>807,160.41</td><td>6.98%</td></tr><tr><td><b>Cyclical regression</b></td><td><b>522.81</b></td><td><b>410,050.28</b></td><td>5.7293%</td></tr></tbody></table></div><aside class="note">Reported error measures are calculated on historical observations; they are not an independent future-period validation.</aside><figure><a href="./assets/ppic-forecast.png"><img src="./assets/ppic-forecast.png" alt="Actual demand versus three forecast methods in the PPIC report"></a><figcaption>Module 1, Figure 3.8 — original forecast comparison, report p. 73. Click to enlarge.</figcaption></figure>
</section>
<section class="chapter" id="aggregate"><div class="sectitle"><span>03</span><h2>Aggregate production planning</h2></div><p>Compared four strategies to balance workforce stability, inventory and modeled cost. The hybrid plan used a baseline workforce of 169 and had the lowest modeled cost.</p><div class="panel"><h3>Total modeled cost (IDR billion)</h3><div class="bars"><div class="barrow"><span>Chase</span><div class="track"><div class="fill " style="width:83.203%"></div></div><b>29.12B</b></div><div class="barrow"><span>Level</span><div class="track"><div class="fill " style="width:83.120%"></div></div><b>29.09B</b></div><div class="barrow"><span>Hybrid</span><div class="track"><div class="fill selected" style="width:83.116%"></div></div><b>29.09B</b></div><div class="barrow"><span>Transportation</span><div class="track"><div class="fill " style="width:93.094%"></div></div><b>32.58B</b></div></div><p class="small">Hybrid is approximately 10.72% lower than the transportation alternative; the gap versus the level plan is much smaller.</p></div>
</section>
